Abstract

Latent world models plan by predicting the outcomes of candidate actions in a learned representation space. In self-predictive models, however, the encoder and predictor are optimized jointly and can co-adapt to latent transitions that are easy to predict in the training distribution but weakly constrained by the physical evolution of the scene. We introduce the cross-predictive JEPA (JEPA-x), which grounds latent dynamics in privileged physical trajectories. JEPA-x treats visual observations and physical states as corresponding views of the same action-conditioned trajectory, advances both through a shared predictor, and matches each prediction to the future representations of both modalities. This encourages the action-conditioned predictor to learn a common transition rule across the two views. Privileged physical state is used only during training, leaving a visual-only model at deployment. Empirical results show that JEPA-x reduces the rollout drift of a newly fitted predictor from 0.361 to 0.104 and increases mean control success from 53.6% to 78.2% on a multi-task suite spanning six evaluation subfamilies. We additionally show that direct full-state regression improves position decodability without improving forecastability or control, indicating that the benefit comes from shaping latent dynamics rather than merely encoding physical variables.

Similar pixels, different physics

A vision-only world model must predict how the scene will evolve from pixels alone. Two observations can look nearly identical to the model while describing different physical states, and the same action then leads to different futures.

Privileged physical state is commonly used to make physical variables readable from a visual latent. We find that this is not enough: what matters for planning is whether the latent evolves predictably under action. We separate three properties:

DecodabilityCan physical variables be read out from a single latent?
ForecastabilityDoes the representation support action-conditioned prediction beyond its co-trained predictor?
Control utilityDo predicted outcomes preserve the distinctions needed to rank candidate actions?

Method

For every visual trajectory, the simulator provides a synchronized trajectory of physical states. JEPA-x treats the two as paired views of the same action-conditioned evolution.

Training: predict both futures from either view

With visual and physical latent histories Z_t^{o}, Z_t^{s} and action chunk a_t, the objective sums all four source–target pairs:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA-}x} = \sum_{m,n\,\in\,\{o,s\}} \big\lVert g_\psi(Z_t^{m}, a_t) - z_{t+1}^{n} \big\rVert_2^2 \;+\; \beta\,\big[\Omega(z^{o}) + \Omega(z^{s})\big]

Within-modal terms keep self-prediction; cross-modal terms require a transition predicted from either history to reach the same future scene in both modalities. Sharing g_\psi encourages both views to expose a common transition rule, which ties the visual latent to how the physical world actually evolves.

Limitations

Our evaluation uses simulation, paired privileged trajectories during training, and a fixed rigid-body state schema. Improved forecastability strengthens one component of rollout-based planning but does not guarantee higher success on every task. Extending the approach to real-world observations and broader physical interactions is future work.
